THE PROBLEM

Why does waste segregation matter?

When different types of waste are mixed together, useful recyclable materials can become harder to recover, while other waste may require different handling. Lack of information can make proper segregation difficult.

Mixed Waste

Putting different waste types in one container can make sorting and recovery more difficult.

E-Waste

Old electronic devices should not simply be treated like ordinary household waste.

Overconsumption

Buying only what we need and choosing reusable items can reduce unnecessary waste.

SORT GUIDE

Where does it belong?

Use this simple guide as a starting point. Follow your local waste-management rules when they differ.

Biodegradable

Food scraps, fruit and vegetable waste, and other biodegradable materials.

Recyclable

Clean paper, cardboard, and suitable recyclable containers.

Residual

Waste that cannot be composted or practically recycled.

Special / E-Waste

Batteries and old electronics need appropriate collection or disposal channels.

    FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

    Questions about waste and EcoSort

    Find quick answers about sorting waste, responsible consumption, and the activities on this website. Select a question to read its answer.

    What is SDG 12?

    SDG 12 stands for Responsible Consumption and Production. It encourages people to use resources wisely, reduce unnecessary waste, and make more sustainable choices.

    Why is waste segregation important?

    Separating waste helps keep recyclable materials cleaner and makes proper collection and handling easier. Some materials, such as batteries, also need special care.

    What are the four waste categories in EcoSort?

    EcoSort uses Biodegradable, Recyclable, Residual, and Special / E-Waste. These are learning categories; always follow the waste collection rules in your area.

    What belongs in biodegradable waste?

    Examples include fruit peels, vegetable scraps, and some garden waste. These can break down naturally, but composting programs may have different rules about what they accept.

    Can every plastic item be recycled?

    No. Whether a plastic item can be recycled depends on its material, condition, and the facilities available locally. Check your community's collection rules before placing it in recycling.

    How should I prepare items for recycling?

    Empty containers and follow the local program's cleaning and sorting instructions. Keeping accepted materials free of food or other waste makes them easier to collect and process.

    What should I do with old batteries or electronics?

    Use an appropriate battery or electronic-waste collection point. Do not put them in ordinary recycling bins; ask your school or local waste office about available collection options.

    What is residual waste?

    Residual waste includes items that cannot be composted or practically recycled through the collection services available to you. Used tissues and certain non-recyclable wrappers are common examples.

    How can students reduce waste at school?

    Bring a reusable bottle when possible, use supplies carefully, avoid unnecessary disposable items, and sort waste using your school's collection guidelines.

    How does the Waste Sorting Simulator work?

    Look at the item shown, choose one of the four waste categories, and read the explanation. There are 10 items in a round, and you can try again when you finish.

    Do I need an account to take the quiz?

    No. You can use the simulator, take the quiz, and read this website without logging in. The quiz saves recent results in this browser when storage is available.

    What does STS have to do with waste management?

    Science helps explain waste and materials, technology provides ways to share information and manage resources, and society shapes the habits that affect our environment.
